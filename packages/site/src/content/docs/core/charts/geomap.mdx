Creates a geographical map with zooming, panning, image tiles, and the ability to layer choropleth paths and coordinate points.

## Choropleth map

Joins data to the `topojson` shapes via `groupBy` and fills each region by its `colorScale` value; `colorScaleConfig`'s `tickFormat` renders the legend ticks as percentages.

<Playground chart="Geomap" code={choroplethMap} controls={["colorScale","colorScaleConfig","ocean","projection","tiles","topojson"]} />

## Bivariate choropleth

Two fields given to `colorScale` color each region by both at once. Each variable is split into quantile bins (3 by default; `colorScaleConfig.bivariateBuckets` takes 2 to 4), and the pair of bins picks a color from a grid: Joshua Stevens' blue–red scheme by default, where adult obesity rises toward red, physical inactivity toward blue, and both together toward the dark corner. The key turns the grid 45° into a diamond, each variable named along its lower edge. Hover a cell to highlight the states in it, or a state to light its cell and read both values in the tooltip. A state missing either value takes the no-data fill.

<Playground chart="Geomap" code={bivariateChoropleth} controls={["colorScale","colorScaleConfig","projection"]} />

## Coordinate point map

Rather than shading regions, this drops a marker per datum: `point` returns each row's `[longitude, latitude]`, and `pointSize` scales the radius between `pointSizeMin` and `pointSizeMax` by `dma_code`.

<Playground chart="Geomap" code={coordinatePointMap} controls={["point","pointSize","pointSizeMin","pointSizeMax"]} />

## Flow map

`links` draws a line between two places for each row, along the great circle between them, so it follows the map's projection. Each link's `source` and `target` is a `[longitude, latitude]` pair or an id: a row with a coordinate `point` links from that point, and any other id (such as a topojson feature's) from the centroid of its shape. `linkSize` sets each link's width, scaled between `linkSizeMin` and `linkSizeMax`; `linkCurve` bows each link into an arc, to the left of its direction, so the flows each way between New York and London stay apart; and `arrows` points each one at its target. Links color by `linkColor` (here the color of the point they leave), take `linkLabel` text, which follows each arc, and show a "Source → Target" tooltip. They sit above the shapes and below the points, and pan and zoom with the map.

<Playground chart="Geomap" code={flowMap} controls={["linkCurve","linkSize","linkSizeMax","linkColor","arrows","linkLabel","projection"]} />

## Globe

A projection that clips to a circle, such as `"geoOrthographic"`, draws a globe. Dragging it turns the globe under the pointer, keeping the place you grabbed beneath it, while the wheel, a pinch, and the zoom buttons zoom; the reset button turns it back to `projectionRotate`. From the keyboard, focus the chart and hold <kbd>Shift</kbd> with the arrow keys: each press turns the globe 10°, <kbd>←</kbd> <kbd>→</kbd> bringing the land to the west or east into view and <kbd>↑</kbd> <kbd>↓</kbd> the land to the north or south, stopping at the poles. A press eases into place (instantly under `reducedMotion`), holding the keys keeps turning, and the plain arrow keys still move between points. Points on the far side are hidden, and links follow the shortest great circle, without `linkCurve`'s lift, so they stay on the sphere.

<Playground chart="Geomap" code={globe} controls={["projectionRotate","renderer"]} />

## Edge bundling

`linkBundle` pulls links that run the same way near each other into shared corridors, so many flows between two regions read as one: here every flight from six European cities to seven North American ones gathers over the Atlantic and fans out at each end. It bundles the links between their projected ends, in place of `linkCurve`'s arc, and `linkBundleConfig` tunes it (see Network's [edge bundling](/core/charts/network/#edge-bundling)). Only a flat map bundles: on a globe, and for a link the projection cuts (across the antimeridian, or between `"geoAlbersUsa"`'s insets), links keep their great-circle paths.

<Playground chart="Geomap" code={edgeBundling} controls={["linkBundle","linkBundleConfig","projection"]} />

## Changing projection

Any d3-geo projection name can be passed to `projection`; `"geoMercator"` gives the familiar cylindrical world view, while `topojsonFilter` drops Antarctica (`id !== "010"`).

<Playground chart="Geomap" code={changingProjection} controls={["projection","topojson","topojsonFilter"]} />

## Only areas with data

An array of ids passed to `topojsonFilter` keeps only those features, so the map draws just the areas with data and zooms to fit them. For data that changes, pass a function over the features instead: `const ids = new Set(data.map(d => d.id))`, then `topojsonFilter: f => ids.has(f.id)`.

<Playground chart="Geomap" code={onlyAreasWithData} controls={["topojsonFilter"]} />

## Changing the tileset

Point `tileUrl` at any XYZ tile server to swap the background imagery. `noKeyTilesets` lists every server that needs no account or API key: change the name in the code to try another. Esri's Canvas layers are the default, Light Gray on a light page and Dark Gray on a dark one. The credit in the corner updates to match, and any other `{z}/{x}/{y}` template works too (Esri's services order it `{z}/{y}/{x}`).

<Playground chart="Geomap" code={changingTileset} controls={[]} />

## Light and dark tiles

`tileUrl` and `ocean` also accept a `{light, dark}` pair, and the defaults are one: Esri Light Gray Canvas on a light backdrop and Dark Gray Canvas on a dark one, with a matching ocean and no-data fill. Geomap reads the backdrop from the page (the nearest background color, or a dark `color-scheme` on a dark system) and redraws when it changes: switch this site between light and dark to see it.

<Playground chart="Geomap" code={lightAndDarkTiles} controls={[]} />

## Changing no data color

`topojsonFill` sets the color for shapes with no matching data (here light pink), so empty regions read differently from those on the `colorScale`.

<Playground chart="Geomap" code={changingNoDataColor} controls={["topojsonFill"]} />

## Custom colors

Override the default color interpolation by passing an explicit ramp to `colorScaleConfig`'s `color` array — here red → orange → yellow → green → blue across the range.

<Playground chart="Geomap" code={customColors} controls={["colorScaleConfig"]} />

## Custom zoom

`fitObject` frames the initial view to a custom TopoJSON object; the two `MultiPoint` corners here define the bounding box the map zooms to fit.

<Playground chart="Geomap" code={customZoom} controls={["fitObject"]} />

## Disable zooming

`zoom: false` turns off panning and scroll-to-zoom, locking the map at its initial extent.

<Playground chart="Geomap" code={disableZooming} controls={["zoom"]} />

## Hiding the color scale

`colorScalePosition: false` hides the legend while regions stay shaded by `colorScale` — useful when the scale is documented elsewhere.

<Playground chart="Geomap" code={hidingTheColorScale} controls={["colorScalePosition"]} />

## Overriding colorscale behavior

The color scale normally auto-detects its type from the data; setting `colorScaleConfig`'s `scale: "linear"` forces a plain linear ramp instead of the automatic choice.

<Playground chart="Geomap" code={overridingColorscaleBehavior} controls={["colorScaleConfig"]} />

## Removing ocean and tiles

Set `ocean: "transparent"` and `tiles: false` to strip the water fill and background map tiles, leaving just the choropleth shapes.

<Playground chart="Geomap" code={removingOceanAndTiles} controls={["ocean","tiles"]} />

## Rendering to canvas

The same choropleth painted with the Canvas backend (`renderer: "canvas"`). Geography, color scale, tooltips, and zoom all work; SVG remains the default.

<Playground chart="Geomap" code={renderingToCanvas} controls={["renderer"]} />

## Point motion trails

Motion trails aren't just for scatter plots — Geomap coordinate points trail too, and are **on by default**. Press **play**: each storm track sweeps a tapering cone from its previous position to the next as the timeline advances, tracing its path across the map. Toggle `renderer` to compare the SVG and Canvas backends.

<Playground chart="Geomap" code={pointMotionTrails} controls={["renderer","time"]} />

## Point size legend

Coordinate points sized by `pointSize` get a size legend in the corner, drawn with the same `pointSizeScale` as the points. The points scale with the map as you zoom in, so the legend keeps its circles the same size and relabels them: at 4× zoom, the largest circle represents a much smaller population than it does unzoomed.

<Playground chart="Geomap" code={pointSizeLegend} controls={["pointSize","pointSizeMin","pointSizeMax","pointSizeScale","sizeLegendConfig"]} />

## Side color scale

A left or right `colorScalePosition` fits the scale between everything above and below the map (a title, a top legend, a timeline), and starts it below the control buttons in the corner on its side, so the buttons never cover the scale's top label.

<Playground chart="Geomap" code={sideColorScale} controls={["colorScalePosition","title"]} />
