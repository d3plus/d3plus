Creates a horizon chart from time series rows (the same rows a LinePlot
takes): each `groupBy` series gets a short row of its own, labeled on the y
axis, with its `y` values cut into equal bands that fold down onto the row's
baseline and are shaded darker the higher they reach. Many series fit in
the height one area chart would take, and the shades still read as values.

## Basic example

Pass the rows a LinePlot would take: `groupBy` names the series, `x` the
time, and `y` the value. Each series' area is cut into 3 bands (`bands`),
and the bands are stacked on top of each other inside the row, darker the
higher they reach. Every row shares one band size: the largest value
divided by `bands`, rounded up to a round number (1, 2, 2.5, or 5 times a
power of ten), so the same shade means the same values in every row and
the key reads in round steps such as "25 – 50". The band size is measured
from all of the data, before the timeline and hidden rows cut it, so the
shades hold still as you step through time. The legend is a key to the
value range of each shade, and hovering one of its entries lights that
shade in every row. Hover the chart to read every row's value at that date
under a crosshair; with `tooltipShared: false`, the tooltip shows only the
row under the pointer.

## Hiding rows

The band key stands for shades rather than series, so the row labels take
the legend's other job: click a row's label to hide its series, and click
it again to bring it back. Shift+click a label to show only that series.
A hidden row keeps its place, with its label dimmed, and the Back button
undoes each change. Hiding rows never changes the band size. Hovering a
label opens a tooltip just past its end with what a click will do.

From the keyboard, Tab steps through the row labels, top to bottom, once
the chart has focus: Enter hides or shows the focused row, Shift+Enter
shows only that row, and each label is announced with what Enter does
("Oslo, Enter to Hide"). The tooltip's hints follow the input in use:
Enter wording while you move by keyboard, click wording once the mouse
moves over the label, and back to Enter with the next key. After each
change the chart announces what happened: "Oslo hidden", "Oslo shown", or
"Oslo highlighted", worded in each language's own order.

## Negative values

Negative values are mirrored upward from the same baseline, drawn in a
contrasting hue. By default the positive bands ramp toward the theme's
colorScale `max` color and the negative bands toward its `min` color, pale
nearest zero and darker away from it (on a dark theme, dim nearest zero
and brighter away from it), so the chart follows dark and custom themes.

## Bands and colors

`bands` sets how many bands each sign is cut into, and `bandSize` fixes the
range of values each band spans, used as given, to compare charts or to
give the key steps of your choosing; values past the top band fill it. `colors` takes a base color to
ramp for `positive` or `negative` values, or a list of colors from the band
nearest zero up.

## Many rows

Rows share the chart's height evenly, so a taller chart gives each series
more room. `rowGap` sets the space between rows, in pixels (1 by default).
Arrow keys move along a row's points and between rows once the chart has
keyboard focus (left of a row's first point is its label), and the table
view lists every row's values.

## Small multiples

`facet` draws one panel per continent. Every panel shares the x axis and
the band size, so shades compare across panels, and each panel draws and
labels only its own series' rows.
