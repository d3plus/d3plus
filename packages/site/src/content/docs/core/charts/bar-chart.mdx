A bar chart draws one bar per row along a discrete axis. When stacked, each
bar's fraction of its stack total is available to tooltip accessors as `share`
(unless the data has its own `share` field).

## Basic example

A minimal grouped bar chart: the two series in `groupBy` share each `x`
position and sit side by side, with each bar's height read from `y`.

<Playground chart="BarChart" code={basic} controls={["x", "y"]} />

## Sorting bars

The discrete `x` axis sorts alphabetically by default; pass an `xSort`
comparator to order the bars by a different field. Here the travel-time
buckets follow their numeric `"ID Travel Time"` instead of their labels.

<Playground chart="BarChart" code={sorting} />

## Stacked bars

Set `stacked: true` to stack the series into one bar per `x` position rather
than grouping them side by side, emphasizing the combined total over
per-series comparison.

<Playground
  chart="BarChart"
  code={stacked}
  controls={["stacked", "barPadding", "groupPadding", "discrete"]}
/>

## Horizontal bars

Run the bars horizontally by setting `discrete: "y"` so the category sits on
the vertical axis and the measured length extends along `x`. Reach for this
when category labels are long or numerous.

<Playground chart="BarChart" code={horizontal} controls={["discrete"]} />

## Population pyramid

Age ranges run up the discrete `y` axis while the `x` accessor negates the
female `Population` so the sexes mirror around zero, and `xConfig.tickFormat`
restores absolute labels with `formatAbbreviate` from `@d3plus/format`. Use it
to read a population's age-and-sex structure at a glance.

<Playground chart="BarChart" code={pyramid} controls={["stacked"]} />

## Custom tooltip

Replace the default hover card with `tooltipConfig`: `title` builds the header
from the datum and `tbody` lists label/value rows. Hover a bar to see the
custom formatting.
