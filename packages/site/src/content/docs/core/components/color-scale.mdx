Creates an SVG color scale based on an array of data.

## Linear scale

The default `scale: "linear"` maps values to a continuous single-hue gradient (blue). A smooth ramp is best when every value matters equally and you want to read magnitude by eye.

<Playground chart="ColorScale" code={linearScale} controls={["scale"]} height={100} />

## Jenks scale

`scale: "jenks"` splits the data into natural-breaks (ckmeans) buckets — discrete steps placed where the data clusters, minimizing within-bucket variance. This is the default for charts (Geomap choropleths) because it reveals grouping in skewed data that a linear ramp flattens.

<Playground chart="ColorScale" code={jenksScale} controls={["scale","buckets"]} height={100} />

## Buckets scale

`scale: "buckets"` divides the domain into equal-width steps — the breakpoints are evenly spaced by value (unlike jenks, which follows the data). Set the count with `buckets`.

<Playground chart="ColorScale" code={bucketsScale} controls={["scale","buckets"]} height={100} />

## Quantile scale

`scale: "quantile"` puts an equal *count* of data points in each bucket, so every color represents the same share of the data. Breakpoints bunch up where values are dense.

<Playground chart="ColorScale" code={quantileScale} controls={["scale","buckets"]} height={100} />

## Log scale

`scale: "log"` spaces the gradient logarithmically, so each order of magnitude gets comparable color range. Use it when values span several powers of ten (all values must share a sign).

<Playground chart="ColorScale" code={logScale} controls={["scale"]} height={100} />

## Diverging scale

When the data spans both sides of the midpoint (default `0`), the scale becomes **diverging** automatically: red (low) → neutral gray → blue (high), so the two directions read as opposites around the center. Move the center with `midpoint`.

<Playground chart="ColorScale" code={divergingScale} controls={["midpoint"]} height={100} />

## Scale function

`scale` also accepts a function that picks the scale on every render. It receives the numeric values the scale is built from (sorted, without null or NaN) and their `[min, max]` extent, and returns a scale name. Here a range wider than 5 gets jenks buckets and a narrower one gets a linear gradient, so new data (from a timeline or a filter) can switch between them.

<Playground chart="ColorScale" code={scaleFunction} controls={["scale","data"]} height={100} />

## Choosing the labels

A gradient, or a bucketed scale drawn as one bar (`bucketAxis: true`), is labeled by an [Axis](/core/components/axis/) that `axisConfig` configures. The axis picks round values to label, wraps long labels, and hides the ones that would crowd their neighbors, keeping the labels at its two ends. To choose the labels yourself, list their values in `labels` (and the values to mark in `ticks`); `tickFormat` writes their text. A bucketed scale drawn as swatches labels every bucket instead, with text from `bucketFormat` and `bucketJoiner`. In a chart, these go in `colorScaleConfig`, as in `colorScaleConfig: {axisConfig: {labels: [200, 6400]}}`.

In a chart, a scale along one of its sides takes at most a third of the chart's width (a scale at the left or right) or a third of its height (top or bottom), its bar, ticks, labels and title together: labels that need more wrap, are cut short, or are left out. A gradient or a bucketed bar keeps to its third on a short chart too: when its labels don't fit beside its title, the title is left out, and when even a line of labels doesn't fit, the bar is drawn without them. At the top or bottom, that's a chart shorter than about 230px for the title and 165px for the labels. `colorScaleConfig: {axisConfig: {maxSize: …}}` sets the room for the labels and title instead, as a number of pixels or a function of that width or height.

A scale drawn as swatches keeps to the same third: its labels wrap onto more lines, and when they still don't fit, they are cut short with "...". Its title wraps within the third too, and is left out first. `colorScaleConfig: {legendConfig: {width: …}}` (or `height`, for a scale at the top or bottom) sets another limit, in pixels.

The scale's own `width` (at the left or right) or `height` (at the top or bottom), as in `colorScaleConfig: {width: 120}`, replaces the third for any kind of scale, keeping it against its edge of the chart. Along the top or bottom, `width` is the scale's length instead, as `height` is at the left or right.

A scale at the top keeps clear of the chart's corner buttons, as a top legend does: it sits between them when it draws as well there, its swatches keeping every label and its bar at least half of its labels, and otherwise drops below them, where it takes at most a third of the chart.

<Playground chart="ColorScale" code={choosingLabels} controls={["scale","bucketAxis"]} height={100} />

## Hover mark

`hover` marks a value on the scale, as a chart does while one of its shapes is hovered: an extra axis tick marks the value on the bar, and a badge reads it where that tick's label would sit. A bucketed bar (`bucketAxis`) also outlines the value's bucket; a scale drawn as swatches draws no mark. `hoverMark(false)` turns it off; `hoverMarkConfig` styles the `tickConfig`, `badgeConfig`, and `outlineConfig`.

<Playground chart="ColorScale" code={hoverMark} controls={["hover","hoverMark","hoverMarkConfig","scale"]} height={100} />

## Bivariate scale

`bivariate: true` colors by two variables at once: `value` returns a `[first, second]` pair for each row, and each variable is split into `bivariateBuckets` quantile bins (2 to 4, default 3), every bin holding about the same number of rows, with tied values sharing one. The pair of bins picks a color from `bivariateColors`, a square grid listed row by row from the low/low corner (each row one bin of the second variable, each column one of the first). It defaults to Joshua Stevens' blue–red 3×3 scheme, the first variable rising toward red and the second toward blue, and a grid of another size is blended bilinearly to fit, so 2×2 keeps its corners and 4×4 blends between its colors. The key turns the grid 45° into a diamond, low/low at the bottom, with `bivariateLabels` naming each variable along its lower edge. A chart sets all but the colors and labels for you when its `colorScale` names two fields (see Geomap's [bivariate choropleth](/core/charts/geomap/#bivariate-choropleth)).
