Creates a wrapped text box for each point in an array of data.

## Basic example

Wraps three strings inside 200px-wide boxes: inline `<i>`, `<u>`, and `<b>` tags render as styled SVG, while the Chinese and Burmese lines show wrapping still works for scripts that don't separate words with spaces.

<Playground chart="TextBox" code={basicExample} controls={["fontSize","lineHeight","width"]} height={200} />

## Font resize

Three equally-sized boxes hold text of very different lengths. With `fontResize: true` each string is scaled between `fontMin` and `fontMax` to fill its box — the single word is sized way up while the long passage shrinks to fit. Toggle `fontResize` off in the controls to see them all snap back to one fixed size.

<Playground chart="TextBox" code={fontResize} controls={["fontResize","fontMax","fontMin"]} height={180} />

## Alignment

`textAnchor` aligns the wrapped lines horizontally within the box (`start`, `middle`, or `end`) and `verticalAlign` places the block at the `top`, `middle`, or `bottom` of its `height`; both accept accessors, so each datum here chooses its own.

<Playground chart="TextBox" code={alignment} controls={["textAnchor","verticalAlign"]} height={150} />

### Justified text

`textAnchor: "justify"` spreads the words of each line across the box so both edges stay straight. The last line of a paragraph, and any line that ends at a newline (`\n`), keeps its natural spacing and sits at the start. Scripts written without spaces, such as Chinese and Japanese, are justified between characters instead. In right-to-left text, lines start at the right edge, and a run of left-to-right words (a name, say) keeps its own order.

<Playground chart="TextBox" code={justify} controls={["textAnchor","fontSize","width"]} height={200} />

### Percentages

A percentage places each line that share of the way across the box: the space left beside a line is split so `"25%"` of it sits before the line. `"0%"`, `"50%"`, and `"100%"` match `start`, `middle`, and `end`. Percentages follow the writing direction, so in right-to-left text `"0%"` is the right edge, like `start`.

<Playground chart="TextBox" code={percentAnchors} controls={["fontSize"]} height={160} />

## Rotation

`rotate` turns the whole box by that many degrees around `rotateAnchor` (its center by default), the same mechanism axes use for slanted tick labels and charts use for vertical bar labels.

<Playground chart="TextBox" code={rotation} controls={["rotate","rotateAnchor"]} height={60} />

## Truncation

Text that does not fit is cut at the last whole line that fits the `height`, and `maxLines` caps the line count regardless of height. The cut line is passed through `ellipsis`, a function `(text, line) => string` that appends `…` by default and can be replaced to end truncated text any other way.

<Playground chart="TextBox" code={truncation} controls={["maxLines","height"]} height={48} />

The ellipsis is measured with the line: when the line and its ellipsis are too wide for the box, words come off the end of the line until they fit.

### Cutting words

By default truncated text only breaks between words, and a first word too wide for the box draws nothing. `truncateWord: true` lets truncation cut a word instead: the word that did not fit (or a first word wider than the box) is cut to the longest start that fits with the ellipsis. It cuts between whole characters, so an emoji or an accented letter is never split.

<Playground chart="TextBox" code={truncateWord} controls={["truncateWord","width"]} height={90} />

## Long words

A single word wider than the box cannot be wrapped. By default the wrapper stops there and truncates; `overflow: true` lets the word run past the box's right edge instead, so nothing is lost when the box is only a sizing hint.

<Playground chart="TextBox" code={longWords} controls={["overflow","width"]} height={80} />

## Font styling

`fontFamily`, `fontWeight`, `fontColor`, and `fontStroke`/`fontStrokeWidth` all take values or accessors. A thin dark stroke around light text is how chart labels stay legible over any fill.
